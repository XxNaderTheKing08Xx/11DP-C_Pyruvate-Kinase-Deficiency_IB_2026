### Arg486 is conserved from mammals to fish; the rat has Gln

UniProt contains reviewed PKLR entries for only four species, all mammals: human, rat, mouse, and dog (UniProt Consortium 2026). BLAST searches restricted to each species found the corresponding full-length RefSeq entries (O'Leary *et al.* 2016): the dog and rat proteins (NP_001243191.1, XP_006232655.1) are identical to Q29536 and P12928, and the mouse protein (NP_038659.2) differs from P53657 at two positions far from residue 486. A second rat entry, XP_087472322.1, from the genome of strain SHRSP, differs from P12928 only at residue 486, where it has Arg instead of Gln.

For more distant vertebrates we used the one-to-one orthologues of human *PKLR* listed by Ensembl (Dyer *et al.* 2025): opossum, platypus, turtle, frog, coelacanth, and zebrafish. Orthologues are genes in different species that descend from one gene of their common ancestor (Koonin 2005). BLAST alone was not enough here, because the best hits in coelacanth and zebrafish are annotated as PKM, the other pyruvate kinase gene. We did not use the chicken: Ensembl lists no *PKLR* orthologue, and all chicken BLAST hits are PKM.

The multiple alignment of the ten sequences (Clustal Omega) shows that human PKLR is 91–93% identical to the mouse, rat, and dog sequences and 68–84% identical to the other six (Table 2). At position 486, nine species have Arg; only the rat has Gln, and no species has Trp (Figure 3). The neighbouring residues (SAQLLS-R-YRPR) are almost identical in all ten species. The dog, our outgroup, separated from the lineage of primates and rodents about 89 million years ago, before primates and rodents separated from each other, about 84 million years ago (Kumar *et al.* 2022). Since the dog has Arg at position 486, with the same codon as human (CGG), Arg is most likely the ancestral residue, and Gln appeared in the rat lineage after rats and mice separated.
